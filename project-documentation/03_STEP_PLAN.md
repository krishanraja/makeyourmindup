# The step plan

Rules before plumbing, and the format settles before the machine that serves it
gets built. **Integrations written today are obsolete the moment the dry runs
change the template.** That is the single most expensive mistake available here,
and an eager session told to build the machine will build straight past it.

## Phase 0: four decisions, and they block everything

| Decision | State |
|---|---|
| The apex domain | **open.** Cover on the apex, publication on `read.` is the recommendation |
| Does lift.the.lid survive | **settled 2026-09-18.** Retired. Standing shape is two formats plus a rotating third |
| Is your.call the device | **settled.** Yes |
| Where the panel lives | **settled 2026-09-19.** Here, `panel/`. Shares the publish bar with the award panel so one standard runs across the business |

## Phase 1: foundations, as code

The data model. The rubric as a tested pure function with round one's findings
encoded so a later change cannot silently undo them. The format contracts read
from `venture_formats`. The kill list as a check rather than a memory. The panel
runner. The repo scaffold, cover statically generated so it survives the back
end being down.

**Done when** a piece of text can be run through the kill list and the panel
from the command line and get a verdict back.

**State 2026-09-19:** kill list done and self-tested. Rubric is data, not yet a
function. Panel is a roster, not yet a runner. Contracts not started.

## Phase 2: the dry runs

Three subjects, one per format, each written three ways on the opening, the
spine and the temperature. Winners rebuilt as written, audio and vertical video.
The panel scores every variant blind before Krish sees any of them, and where he
and the panel disagree is the judges' first calibration data.

**Done when the template is fixed. Everything downstream waits on this, which is
the whole reason it sits here.**

**State 2026-09-19:** one subject of three picked. See
`calibration/2026-09-17-dry-run-picks.json`.

## Phase 3: the migration

Point brain at CTRL and verify before removing the old domain. Park the
diagnostic, dated and marked not live. Point `read.` at Substack. Redirect the
old links. Write the domain constraint into the CTRL naming gate so nobody reads
a URL as the product name.

**Done when** all three domains resolve and nothing 404s. Approved per action.

## Phase 4: the cover

A magazine front at the apex. Wordmark, line, the formats, current issue, the
verdict archive when it exists, one route into the publication. Static, so a
dead back end never takes it down.

**Gate:** Krish's material approval, shown without commentary, plus Art
Director 8 or above.

## Phase 5: the machine

Watch, score, assemble, dispatch, in that order, research and sourcing first.

**The rule:** if a job adds to the two to four hours a piece, it is wrong
regardless of output quality. **Done when** a scored Sunday slate arrives
without anyone asking for it.

## Phase 6: the Control Center

Screens only, no logic. The Sunday board first, because Sunday is the last day
the whole week can still be saved. Then the week view, the verdict archive, the
themes tracker, metrics in the order success was actually defined, the bad-week
switch, and the panel's findings surfaced where the work is.

**Gate:** phase 5. Screens with nothing behind them teach nothing.

## Phase 7: Substack housekeeping

Rename the sections, create the new one, rewrite About as the manifesto, replace
the preview image still carrying a retired wordmark, unpublish the weak archive,
rewrite the best three to five as launch stock, set the paid tier.

**Gate:** phase 2, for anything needing the settled voice.

## Phase 8: bank four

Canva, the R&D department, the calculator kids, 2,500 years. These are the four
Krish rated excellent, recorded in
`calibration/2026-09-17-commissioning-round-1.json`.

**This is the real test of the whole system.** Four pieces end to end proves the
machine, the panel, the template and the time budget in one go. If it takes
eight hours a piece, something above is wrong, and it is far better to find that
out here than in launch week.

**Gate:** every piece clears the publish bar.

## Phase 9: launch

Manifesto as the first issue, a week of daily posts from the bank, then the
standing cadence. Timed so the site's media route points at a live channel.

## Phase 10: steady state

Three things compound, and they are the reason any of this is worth building:
the mind-change rate, which nobody else has; the commissioning rubric, sharper
every week against Krish's votes; and the panel, sharper every week against his
overrides.

**First review** four weeks after launch, against invitations and quotes,
whether he still enjoys it, and whether paid covers the running cost. Not
impressions.
