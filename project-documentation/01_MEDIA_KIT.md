# Media kit v1.0

Derived from 63 stack-ranked questions across six interactive rounds on
17 September 2026. Where a note contradicted a ranking, the note won, because it
is the more considered statement.

**Supersedes:** Built with AI, The Money of AI, Mindmaker Live,
inspect.the.build, follow.the.money, Newsflash.

## The north star, in his words

> Promoting high human agency is the key mission for me as a person, so that
> people do not just get used by AI, but use it to improve themselves, to make
> their minds better. Pre-AI era leadership isn't going to cut it, delegating
> like they used to won't cut it, and expecting AI to be smarter than you is
> exactly what will make it smarter than you.

**It never appears as a statement.** It reaches the page through what gets
chosen to inspect and through the mechanics of the format, and nowhere else.

## The reader

One reader, written to directly: **a senior leader in a business doing roughly
five to fifty million pounds who will not admit, to anyone, that they are not
ready for what is happening.**

Behind them, ranked: the curious professional keeping up without the jargon, the
investor deciding what to fund and what to fix, the founder at a fork. Two were
demoted on the record: content, media and publishing ranked fifth, and the
builder wanting permission ranked last, which formally ends the audience that
Built with AI served.

## What it is for, in priority order

1. **Top of funnel for mind/make.** The commercial job, ranked first.
2. **Public proof that the operator opens the machine** rather than talking
   about it.
3. **Its own revenue**, through paid subscriptions.

The funnel is indirect: purpose ranked funnel first while success ranked direct
conversion fifth. Nobody optimises this channel for click-through.

## How a reader should feel

Ranked: **entertained, because the accurate line was also the funny one**, then
relieved that someone explained it plainly, then slightly alarmed and equipped.
"Smarter than the room" came last of six.

## The enemy

Never a named person. Vendor hype that sells transformation without production
evidence, then business press that reports the announcement and never traces the
money, then consultants who rename what the client already knows.

Plus his own addition, which is the internal enemy and where the machinery will
drift if left alone: **being boring, overly corporate, "today's headlines in
AI".**

## Voice

**The channel speaks as "we", to "you".** Krish is the byline, the voice on
audio, the face on camera. First person survives only on his own LinkedIn posts
and in audio and video intros. Not in Opinions, not on the About page, not in an
editor's note.

Two registers, split by surface:

- **Substack and LinkedIn.** Energetic and optimistic with real rigour
  underneath. Not Boring for energy and diagrams, Stratechery for the calm
  seriousness of the mechanism, Money Stuff for wit that lives inside the
  mechanism.
- **YouTube and TikTok.** His words: really easy to follow, relatable to
  everyone, thought provoking and really fun to consume. The scrappy energy
  lives here and does not leak into the written channel.

### The rule that overrides everything else

> We should not sound preachy or like we are trying to persuade anyone of
> anything like a missionary in content. Thought leadership should do that job.

A piece that ends by telling the reader what this means for their leadership has
failed, whatever else it did well.

### Fixed rules

- British English, plain enough for a twelve-year-old, every business term
  explained the moment it appears.
- No em dashes anywhere, including on-screen text and code.
- Irony points at claims, hype, incentives and decisions. Never at a person.
- Headlines are short, dry and format-branded. "[Subject], inspected." is the
  pattern.
- Openings name the subject in the first line. No warm-up, no rhetorical
  question.

## The formats

**`venture_formats.mandate` in Mindmaker OS is the only truth for what each
format is.** Read it from there. What follows is the shape, not the mandate.

**Three standing subchannels.** Ruling (Krish, 2026-09-19), reversing the
2026-09-18 retirement of lift.the.lid. One table, because two tables in one
section is how a reader ends up choosing which one to believe.

| Slug | Label | Day | The question your.call asks | Diagram |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `split_the_bill` | split.the.bill | Wednesdays | What does it really cost to run, and who ends up holding the bill? | the money map |
| `mind_the_gap` | mind.the.gap | Fridays, the hero | What is actually happening, against what everyone says is happening? | the timeline |
| `lift_the_lid` | lift.the.lid | No fixed day | Does this make its user sharper, or dependent? | the annotated product shot |

The slug is what the database stores and what every system writes. The label is
what a reader sees. They are never interchangeable in code.

The earlier table in this section gave split.the.bill's your.call question as
"is the money still here in eighteen months". The live mandate has that as its
THIRD question, under the standing one above. The kit had promoted it, which is
the kind of drift a file copy makes and a read from the table does not.

`lift_the_lid` publishes when a subject earns it rather than on a fixed slot,
because a third fixed day costs hours the two-to-four-hour rule does not have.

**The boundary between `split_the_bill` and `lift_the_lid` is the question,
never the surface.** Both can be handed the same pricing page. What it costs and
who pays is `split_the_bill`. Sharper or dependent is `lift_the_lid`. Product
surfaces are `lift_the_lid`'s own territory and are evidence, never a subject,
for `split_the_bill`.

The calibration is worth keeping in view rather than treating as settled:
across four lift.the.lid ideas in
`calibration/2026-09-17-commissioning-round-1.json` Krish gave no excellent at
all, while the other two took four between them. One round of fifteen votes is
a direction, not a verdict, and the five newer lift.the.lid ideas were never
judged.

Six themes tracked across all of them over time: distribution, buyer versus
user, the labour shape, data rights, trust, pricing.

## The device: your.call

**Every signature piece opens with a small interactive artifact.** Not a takeout
at the end. It carries the subject, the claim being tested, and two to four
pieces of evidence, and it asks the reader the format's question **before** they
read. The reader makes a call. Then they read. At the end they can change it,
and they see what other readers called and how many changed their mind.

His note, which is the reason it is built this way:

> I wonder if the artifact acts as a teaser to the full deep dive instead of
> being the takeout. I am fine with a verdict, but I prefer arming people with
> the tools to create their own verdict, rather than trying to persuade them of
> my opinion.

**It cannot preach.** The reader supplies the verdict, so the hardest constraint
is satisfied by construction rather than by discipline. And it makes the name
literal: makeyourmindup is what the reader does.

### What compounds

**The mind-change rate.** Every other AI publication can count subscribers and
opens. Only this one would know, per theme and over quarters, what senior
leaders believed before the evidence, what they believed after, and how often
the evidence moved them.

Collect from day one, show nothing until roughly a hundred responses a piece.
**What would falsify it:** under one in five readers engaging after month one
means it is decoration, and the device reverts to a written verdict.

## The week, and what a piece actually is

| | |
|---|---|
| Sunday | Commission the week from what the pipeline surfaced |
| Wednesday | split.the.bill: piece, artifact, diagram, audio, vertical cut |
| Friday | mind.the.gap: piece plus Resources folded in, artifact, diagram, audio, vertical cut |

Plus one narrative film a month. A signature piece is a package, not a post:
1,500 to 2,500 words, the opening your.call artifact, one diagram in the
format's own language, the audio version, and the vertical cut published the
same day.

### The number everything else protects

**Two to four hours of his time per signature piece, including recording.**
Nothing else was acceptable. Any workflow that adds to his hours is wrong
regardless of how good the output is.

**Templating is deliberately minimal for now.** The only element fixed is the
same-day vertical cut. Everything else waits for the dry runs. Do not impose a
template before then.

## Free and paid

- **Free to everyone:** the written signature pieces in full, and the shorts.
  These carry reach and pickup and are never gated.
- **Paid, eight to ten pounds a month:** CTRL beta access, artifacts and
  diagrams as reusable files, audio and video versions, a monthly live session,
  the full Resources library and the verdict archive.

CTRL beta access is the only visible connection between the channel and
mind/make. That is deliberate.

## The kill list

Executable at `scripts/qa/kill-list.mjs` against
`quality/panel/kill-list.v1.json`. Never allowed:

- Today's headlines roundups with no verdict
- Anything that reads like a vendor pitch
- AI safety, ethics or regulation commentary
- A joke at a named person
- Jargon a twelve-year-old could not follow
- Mindmake or CTRL inside the editorial
- A signature piece without a diagram
- A closing moral, a lesson for leaders, or any sentence telling the reader what
  to believe

Allowed: a prediction without a date; tutorials and how-tos; a load-bearing
number without a primary source, **provided the number still names whoever
produced it**.

> **One recorded disagreement.** Krish ranked the unsourced number as allowed.
> It goes in his way, narrowed to attribution. The register only works if the
> evidence handling is straight, and this is the one line in this kit where the
> author disagrees with the owner on the record.

## What success looks like

1. **It gets picked up.** Ranked within that: a podcast or a stage invites him
   because of a piece, then a journalist quotes a piece, then senior people
   reshare with their own comment.
2. He still enjoys making it and the machinery does most of the grind.
3. Paid subscriptions cover the machinery's running costs.
4. Subscriber growth without pushing it on LinkedIn.

Direct conversion to mind/make ranked fifth and is not a headline metric.
**Control Center tracks invitations and quotes, not impressions**, plus the
mind-change rate, subscriber growth, paid conversion, and running cost against
paid revenue.

## The bad week rule

The engine ships Resources automatically no matter what. A digest-only week is
acceptable. **One signature piece plus Resources is the floor. Skipping silently
is not.**
